How to Measure

Three widths, three heights, both diagonals, and the depth. Smallest number wins, then take off a half inch. That is the whole method.

Do not measure a window you have not opened up yet

Every number on this page is taken off bare wood, with the sashes out, the parting bead out, the inside stops off, and the pocket scraped clean. Measuring around a sash, a bead, or an old insert is the single most expensive mistake on this job — the window shows up too small and it is nobody's fault but the tape's.

1. Width — three readings, jamb to jamb

Measure the clear opening between the two side jambs at the top, the middle, and the bottom. Old jambs bow. They are almost never the same three times.

Hook the tape on the jamb, not the stop

You are measuring the wood the window will actually screw into. If your tape is catching on a stop, a bead, or a chunk of old caulk, you are measuring the wrong thing.

2. Height — three readings, from the high point of the sill

Measure at the left, the centre, and the right, from the underside of the head jamb down to the sill. Because the sill slopes, always come down to its highest point — the back edge, nearest the room.

Why the high point matters

3. Square — measure both diagonals

Corner to corner, both ways. If the two numbers match, the opening is square and the window will drop in and operate. If they do not, you already know you will be shimming and scribing — and if it is bad enough, you order tighter to buy yourself room.

Diagonal differenceWhat it meansWhat you do
Under ¼″Square enough.Order normally. Standard shimming.
¼″ to ½″Noticeably out.Order normally but expect to shim hard on one side and scribe the stops.
Over ½″Badly racked.Flag it. Consider ordering an extra ¼″ down, and warn the customer the trim will need scribing.

4. Depth — will an insert even fit?

With the parting bead out, measure from the face of the blind stop to the face of the inside stop. A standard insert wants about 3¼″. Check the spec sheet for the unit you actually sell — some run as little as 2¾″.

If the pocket will not make depth, stop. That is a full-frame replacement, and it needs to be priced as one before anyone talks to the customer about money. There is a drawn-out section on this in Anatomy & Terms.

5. The deduction — half an inch, every time

Take ½″ off the width and ½″ off the height, off the smallest reading in each direction. That gap is what lets you shim the unit square in an opening that is not, and it is what you pack with insulation afterwards. It is not slop — it is the working room the install depends on.

Round down, never up

If your arithmetic lands between eighths, go to the smaller eighth. A window a sixteenth too small gets shimmed. A window a sixteenth too big goes back on the truck.

6. Write it down before you leave the room

Six readings, two diagonals, one depth, per opening — and every opening in the house is its own set. Nobody remembers that walking down a ladder.

  • Width at top, middle, bottom
  • Height at left, centre, right
  • Both diagonals
  • Pocket depth
  • A name for the opening you will still understand next week — “kitchen, over the sink”, not “window 4”
  • Anything odd: rot, a bowed jamb, a sill that is shot, paint you suspect is lead
