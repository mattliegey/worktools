Both paths

Setting the New Window

From here the two paths are the same job. A clean pocket, a square unit, and a seal that will still be there in twenty years.

Check the window against the opening before you open a tube of anything

Unwrap it, check the label size against your worksheet, look it over for shipping damage, and offer it up to the opening dry. Finding out the unit is wrong is a bad morning. Finding out after it is foamed in is a bad week.

Bed the sill, set the unit, shim it square

The sill is where every failed insert leaks. Lay a continuous bead — two if the sill is worn — and set the unit into it while it is still wet so it squeezes out and seals.

Operate the sashes before you finish fastening

Run both sashes all the way up and down, and throw the lock. If a sash drags or the lock will not line up, the frame is racked — back the screws off and re-shim now. Once it is foamed and trimmed, your only fix is taking it apart again.

Insulate — low expansion, and only low expansion

The shim gap gets insulated, not stuffed. Use foam labelled for windows and doors. The regular high-expansion stuff will keep growing in a gap it cannot escape, and the only direction left is into your jamb.

Seal it, outside and in

Two different jobs. Outside keeps weather out. Inside stops air moving through the assembly. Both matter, and neither one covers for the other.

WhereWhatWatch for
Exterior perimeterExterior-grade sealant between the new frame and the blind stop or exterior trim, all four sides.Tool it into the joint. A bead sitting proud of the surface peels within a couple of seasons.
HeadSet the expander down to the head jamb and seal behind it.This is the joint that gets skipped, and it is directly in the path of anything running down the wall.
SillThe bed you already laid, plus a tooled exterior joint.Never seal the drainage weeps shut. Water that gets in has to get back out.
Interior perimeterPaintable sealant between frame and jamb, behind where the stop lands.Do it before the stops go on, not after.

Stops back on, and test it like the customer will

  • Trim any cured foam flush with a knife
  • Put the original stops back on the side they came from, and nail beside the old holes, not into them
  • Fill the nail holes, caulk the trim joints, touch up the paint
  • Run both sashes fully open and fully shut, several times
  • Throw the lock — it should meet without you pushing the sash sideways
  • Tilt both sashes in and back if it is a tilt unit, and check they latch
  • Check the screens fit and clip in
  • Peel every label and clean the glass
  • HEPA vac the room, and bag the debris if this was a lead job
  • Walk the customer through operating and cleaning it before you leave
The lock is the tell

A lock that needs a shove is the window telling you it is not square. It will not settle in on its own, and it is the number one thing you get called back for.
