Anatomy & Terms

Every instruction on this site uses these words. Ten minutes here saves an hour of confusion on the wall.

The window, part by part

Step through it below. On install day the frame stays in the wall, the sashes and beads come out, and only the inside stops come off — all the other trim stays undisturbed.

The pocket — the part that decides everything

Cut horizontally through the window and look down at one side jamb. This is the view that matters, because the space between the inside stop and the blind stop is the pocket — the slot your new insert has to live in. If it is too shallow, an insert will not work here at all.

Check the pocket before you quote the job

A shallow pocket is the one thing that stops an insert dead. Measure it on the first visit, before anyone promises the customer a price. If it will not make depth, the honest answer is a full-frame replacement.

What the new unit brings with it

An insert is a complete window in its own frame. We're putting a new window inside the old window. It has no nailing fin — it is built to be screwed through its jambs into your old frame and covered by the stops.

What the extender and the expander are actually for

The unit does not fill the opening, and it was never meant to. It sits on the old sill at the inside edge and the rest of its sill is out over thin air, because the old sill slopes away underneath it. The height clearance you deducted ends up as a gap at the head. Neither of those shows face-on. Cut the window down the middle and both are obvious — along with the two pieces of trim that deal with them.

Terms, in one place

TermWhat it isWhy you care
Side jambThe vertical board on each side of the frame.Width is jamb to jamb, so what the stops hide gets added back. This is the surface your screws land in.
Head jambThe horizontal board at the top.Height is measured up to this. The head expander closes the gap here.
SillThe sloped board at the bottom, outside the stool.It slopes, so heights get measured from its high point.
CasingThe trim around the opening — head and both sides.Stays on the wall. Score the paint where the stop meets it so you do not tear it.
Inside stopRemovable strip holding the lower sash in from the room side.First thing off on install day, last thing back on. It is also what hides part of your opening when you measure. Save it — new stock rarely matches.
Parting beadThin strip in a groove separating the two sashes.Comes out on install day. It does not block your depth reading — the tape hook rides past it.
Blind stopFixed strip at the exterior edge of the jamb.The outer limit of the pocket, and what the new unit pushes up against.
PocketThe clear space between blind stop and inside stop.If it is under about 3¼″, an insert will not fit.
StoolThe interior shelf — what most people call the sill.Stays put. Your unit lands behind it, not on it.
ApronTrim under the stool.Only comes off if you are replacing the stool.
Sill extenderA fin that clips into the accessory groove and hangs below the unit's sill.Hides the open space left under the outer half of the sill. Detents along the extrusion let you snap it to height.
Head expanderAn upside-down U packed with fiberglass, closing the gap at the head.Optional. Plenty of crews leave it off and cap the exterior in aluminum instead.
Accessory grooveA slot running around the exterior face of the unit's frame.What the extender, and anything else that clips on, hooks into.
Insert / pocket windowA complete window built to drop into an existing frame.What this whole site is about. No nailing fin, no siding work.
Full frameTearing the old frame out back to the rough opening.The fallback when the pocket is too shallow or the frame is rotten.
