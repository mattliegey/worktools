Path A

Removing the Original Sashes

The original wood double-hung is still intact. Everything inside the frame comes out — stops, sashes, bead, weights — before a tape ever touches the opening.

Built before 1978? Then this is a lead job

Prying painted stops and sliding painted sashes makes lead dust, and old windows are where most of it comes from. Certified renovator on site, poly down and taped, the room sealed off, no dry scraping or sanding, HEPA vac only, and the paperwork signed. If you are not set up for that today, do not open the window.

Every job, lead or not: eye protection, cut gloves on any sash with glass in it, and never leave a loose sash standing in the opening.

What you need

  • Utility knife, fresh blade
  • Stiff putty knife
  • Flat bar / trim pry bar
  • Nippers or end cutters
  • HEPA vacuum
  • Tape measure

Score the inside stop before you touch it with a bar

The stop is painted to the jamb and to the casing. Cut both of those paint lines with a sharp knife first. Skip this and you tear the paint off the casing, split the stop, or both — and you need that stop back on at the end of the day.

Pull the nails through the back

Do not hammer them back out the face — that blows the wood out around every hole. Grab the shank from the back with nippers and pull it straight through.

Lower sash out, then the parting bead, then the upper

That order is not optional. The parting bead traps the upper sash, and the lower sash is in the way of the parting bead.

Spiral balances are under tension

If the window has spiral or block-and-tackle balances instead of cords and weights, they are wound tight. Release them the way the maker intended, or unscrew the top anchor with the sash still supported. Letting one snap back takes fingers.

Pull the weights and insulate the pockets

Those cavities run the full height of the window on both sides and they are empty. It is the cheapest insulation you will ever install, and the window is never open like this again.

Prep the pocket, and be honest about what you find

The pocket has to be clean, sound, and square-ish before you measure it. This is also the moment you find out whether the job you quoted is the job you have.

  • Pull every nail, staple, cord pulley and bit of hardware out of the jambs
  • Scrape the jambs and sill flat — wet-scrape only if there is any chance of lead
  • Vacuum the pocket out with the HEPA, sill corners included
  • Probe the sill and the bottom of both jambs with an awl. Soft wood is rot, and rot is a conversation with the customer, not something you foam over
  • Fill the old nail holes and any gouges deep enough to matter
  • Prime bare wood, especially the sill
  • Check the pocket depth now — before anyone orders anything
Rot is a stop-work item

An insert screws into the jambs and lands on the sill. If either is punky, there is nothing holding your window in and nothing keeping water out. Repair it properly or price the opening as a full-frame. Do not shim over it and hope.

Now measure

Bare wood, stops off, bead out, pocket clean. This is the only state in which the numbers mean anything.

How to measure Three widths, three heights, both diagonals, the depth. Worksheet Punch the readings in, get the order size.
